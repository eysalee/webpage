<h4>Course Description</h4>
<p>
  The study of discrete mathematics provides an important foundation for basic theoretical principles in computer science. We first build a strong foundation in logic, formal proofs, and mathematical induction. We then examine the discrete structure of functions, relations, and graphs, which provide different ways of representing relationships, connections, and networks. Finally, we move onto numerical and computational aspects: number theory and modular arithmetic, counting and combinatorics, and discrete probability.
</p>

<h5>Learning Outcomes</h5>
<ul>
  <li>Build a strong foundation for all of mathematics in proofs and logic.</li>
  <li>Apply rigorous proof techniques, including direct proofs, proof by counterexample, proof by contradiction, and proof by induction.</li>
  <li>Manipulate and describe relationships between sets of objects using relations and functions.</li>
  <li>Understand basic concepts in graph theory and their usage in modeling a variety of computer science problems.</li>
  <li>Understand basic concepts in number theory and their application to cryptography.</li>
</ul>

<h5>Grading</h5>
<p>
  This course involves written homework assignments, two exams, and in-class assignments. Final grades are based on the instructor's holistic evaluation of your performance and follow <a href="https://catalog.barnard.edu/barnard-college/academic-policies-procedures/grading-academic-honors/" target="_blank">Barnard's grading system</a>.
  As a general guideline, assignments are weighted as follows:
  <ul>
    <li>
      <b>Homework</b> (30%):  There will be weekly written homework assignments. Homework assignments must be typed. You may use use Word, LaTeX, or other text processing, but you may not submit a scan of a handwritten document. Solutions must be written individually. You may not use generative AI for your assignments.
    </li>
    <li><b>Midterm</b> (30%): The first exam will cover material from the first half of the course.</li>
    <li><b>Final Exam</b> (30%): The final exam is non-cumulative and will cover material from teh second half of the course.</li>
    <li><b>Quizzes</b> (10%): Throughout the semester we will have in-class quizzes. The quizzes are meant to be checks of understanding and will cover basic concepts from the lecture and/or previous lecture.</li>
  </ul>
</p>


<h4>Course Policies</h4>
<h5>Collaboration</h5>
<p>Students may discuss problems with one another and work toward solutions in small groups of their choosing (max 4 people). However, <b>every student must individually write and submit solutions to the homework problems assigned themselves</b>. The point of homework discussions is to help you understand the material. It is not acceptable to simply swap solutions, and every student should be able to orally explain every solution they submit. <b>Looking at written homework solutions from the internet or other people is prohibited (this includes letting other students read your answers)</b>.</p>

<h5>Generative AI Policy</h5>
<p>
  <b><span style="color:#DC143C;">The use of generative AI for assignments is prohibited in this class</span></b>. You may use AI as a study tool to find practice problems or explain concepts, but you should not be using AI to solve homework assignments for you.
  <b>It is also always forbidden to incorporate any substantive portion of an AI resource's output into your answers</b>, even if you paraphrase it or rewrite it in your own words. If you think that the instructor might refuse to perform a certain task for you or answer a certain question during office hours, then you should definitely not be asking an AI resource to perform the same task or answer the same question.
</p>

<h5>Re-grade Requests</h5>
  <p>Any requests for regrades of assignments must be received within 2 weeks after the grade was received.  If you request a regrade, we reserve the right to lower your grade if, upon re-review of your assignment, the original grading was found to be too generous.  Any requests for regrades after this timeframe will not be considered.</p>

<h5>Late Policy</h5>
  <p>Assignments may be submitted up to 5 days after the deadline for a penalty: 
  <ul>
    <li>Less than 24 hours: 5% deduction</li>
    <li>Between 1 and 2 days: 10% deduction</li>
    <li>Between 2 and 3 days: 15% deduction</li>
    <li>Between 3 and 4 days: 20% deduction</li>
    <li>Between 4 and 5 days: 25% deduction</li>
  </ul>
